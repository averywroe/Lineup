      How to play

      You get an MLB team and a season from 2006–2010. Rebuild the starting lineup the team used most often, in batting order, in three attempts.

      • Type a name and pick from the list (every MLB player is searchable). Pick a position for each row.
      • Use ▲▼ to move a row, or tap a batting number and then another to swap two rows. Name and position move together.
      • A row locks when player, position and batting slot are all right. You can edit everything else.
      • Missing players or positions, or duplicates, don't use an attempt.
      • AL puzzles use designated-hitter lineups. NL puzzles keep the pitcher in his real batting slot. Any pitcher with 5+ starts for that team that season counts as the pitcher.

      Feedback

      Each row gets three separate marks: one for the player, one for his position, and one for his batting slot.

      ✓Player: right player, right slot. Position: right position for that player (even if he's in the wrong slot). Order: exact slot.
      ↑Player is in the lineup but bats elsewhere. ↑ means earlier, ↓ means later.
      ½Position: a documented secondary position (5+ games that season), or a non-starter's usual role with the team.
      ≈Order: one or two slots away.
      ±3Player played for this franchise within three seasons before or after (inclusive), but not this season.
      RPlayer was on this season's roster but isn't in the lineup.
      ✗No match. A dash (–) means it doesn't apply.

      Answer keys

      Each answer is the exact nine-man combination (players, positions, batting order) that started most often in that season's Retrosheet game logs, with ties going to the earliest one. AL lineups count DH games only; NL lineups skip DH games and treat lineups that differ only by starting pitcher as the same lineup. The Daily changes at midnight Eastern.
